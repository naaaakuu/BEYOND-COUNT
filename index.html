साङ्ख्य • Sāṅkhya

COUNTABLE

● 18 Pink Balls

Small Pink Balls: Separate, distinct objects with clear borders. You can count them one by one: 1, 2, 3... to N.

Jain Canon: Sāṅkhyeya (Finite, countable matter)
असंख्येय • Asaṅkhyeya

UNCOUNTABLE

〰️ Cotton Fibers (Resha)

Tangled Cotton (Resha): Thousands of fine intertwining micro-filaments. Though limited in weight, they cannot practically be counted one by one.

Jain Canon: Asaṅkhyeya (Too tangled to enumerate)
अनन्त • Ananta

INFINITE

✦ Ganga-valuka (Endless)

Endless Sand / Dirt: Continuous streaming golden grains. Like the sands of sacred rivers (Ganga-valuka), expanding without start or end.

Jain Canon: Ananta (Boundless infinity)

CARBON DATING MADE SIMPLE

When an ancient Indian artifact is dug up after thousands of years, how do scientists know its exact age? They count the silent decay of Carbon-14 atoms!

🔍 Tap to view full diagram
Carbon-14 Cycle (Half-Life: 5,730 yrs) Tap Image to Zoom
☀️

1. Sun & Air Make Carbon-14

Cosmic rays from space hit nitrogen atoms in the upper atmosphere, turning them into radioactive Carbon-14.

🌿

2. Living Things Breathe It In

Plants take it in through photosynthesis, animals eat plants. As long as anything is alive, it stays 100% full of Carbon-14.

⏳

3. At Death, Intake Stops

When a tree is cut or an animal dies, intake stops. Carbon-14 slowly decays away: exactly half disappears every 5,730 years!

🔬

4. Count What's Left = Know The Age

Scientists count the remaining Carbon-14 atoms. 50% left? It died 5,730 years ago! 25% left? It died 11,460 years ago!

Ancient Jain Mathematics

1. Sāṅkhyeya (Countable) Things with distinct boundaries that can be counted sequentially.
2. Asaṅkhyeya (Uncountable) Quantities like tangled threads or leaves in a forest—finite, yet beyond human counting.
3. Ananta (Infinite) Like the endless grains of river sand, boundless with no beginning or end.
